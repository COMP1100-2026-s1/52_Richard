Brian: Hello, my name is Brian, and I’m doing a project for Software Innovation. I’m researching how students decide which courses to take. This interview will be recorded. Is that okay with you?

Interviewee: Yeah, sure.

Brian: Okay. This interview will only take around 5 to 7 minutes. For the first question, how did you decide which courses to take this semester?

Interviewee: Based on my previous experience, I usually take the ones that match my interests. Let’s say I’m a Computer Science student and I really like cybersecurity, then I would choose courses like cloud computing and other cybersecurity-related subjects that support me.

Brian: Based on what you said, what is the main thing that affects your decision? Is it your personal interest?

Interviewee: First of all, yes, obviously my personal interest, because I want to take cybersecurity. And if I get an elective, I will probably choose one that interests me but is also easy to learn. For example, if it is fast-paced, I want to balance my courses so that every semester does not get too hard.

Brian: Can you walk me through the last time you had to pick an elective course? What did you do? Where did you look for information, and who did you ask?

Interviewee: For elective courses, I asked one of my senior friends. I asked them, “What’s a good elective that supports cybersecurity?” They said criminology is good, and it is also easy to learn. So I took criminology, and right now it makes things more manageable because criminology taught me useful things for cybersecurity, but it is also very easy to learn.

Brian: Based on the courses that you have taken, can you name one course that you finished recently and explain what skills you developed or gained from that course?

Interviewee: Let’s say cloud computing. For me, that was a really great experience because I learned cloud-related skills.

Brian: And what skills did you gain from it?

Interviewee: What I gained from cloud computing was learning about Oracle programs and also things like DNS servers and stuff like that.

Brian: So basically, from that course, you gained skills that allow you to do things involving cloud computing or databases. Is that right?

Interviewee: Yeah, basically like that.

Brian: Do you have a rough idea of what jobs or roles you want to do after graduating?

Interviewee: To be honest, not yet, but I want to do cybersecurity, mainly focusing on databases. First of all, I like databases, and I also study cybersecurity. So I think those two will fit the kind of job I want in the future.

Brian: Do you feel confident that your current course choices are leading you there?

Interviewee: Yeah, pretty much. Besides taking courses, I also join multiple events so I do not get bored with only course material and so I can have more social life. For example, cybersecurity events or Capture the Flag tournaments. That helps me maintain my skill level in cybersecurity.

Brian: Imagine you had a time machine that could take you back to your first semester. Knowing what you know now, what would you do differently about how you chose your courses? Is there a course that you regret taking or wish you had replaced?

Interviewee: Yeah, maybe with elective courses. In the first semester, I took electives like economics, which are not really relevant to what I’m focusing on right now. So that is one thing. Also, maybe in the first year I would try criminology earlier and then see what I was capable of doing.

Brian: For the final question, can you think about how you chose courses in your first year versus how you choose them now? Has what mattered to you shifted? And if so, what caused that shift?

Interviewee: Basically, in the first year, I was kind of lost. I had no clue about choosing courses. I had no senior friends. What changed was that now I have some senior friends, including one senior in cybersecurity who is now taking a master’s degree. So I kind of followed his path, and that is what influenced me.

Brian: So basically, the difference between your first year and now is that you have senior friends you can rely on or use as an example?

Interviewee: Exactly.

Brian: Okay, that’s all for this interview. Thank you very much.

Interviewee: Adios.
