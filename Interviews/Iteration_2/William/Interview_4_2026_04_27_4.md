**Me:** Hi, can I interview you for around 5 to 10 minutes regarding my project?

**Interviewee:** Yeah.

**Me:** I would like to inform you beforehand that this interview will be recorded.

**Interviewee:** Alright.

**Me:** First question: walk me through how you decided which course you wanted to take next semester. What did you do step by step?

**Interviewee:** I mostly talked to my mates and looked at the plan that they put up, and that’s mostly how I did it.

**Me:** So you mainly followed how your friends chose their courses?

**Interviewee:** Yes.

**Me:** When you are planning your courses across your whole degree, how do you figure out what order makes sense? Some people feel that they should take the harder courses first and easier courses in the later semesters, while some people prefer taking easier courses first and harder courses later.

**Interviewee:** I am very boring. I like to do the easy courses first, and then I like to do the harder courses later. I looked at the plan that they give out, which is very useful because you can see what courses they want you to do and what you have to do. At the beginning of it, they also inform you what courses to choose, such as how first-year courses start with one, second-year courses start with two, and so on. Following those guidelines, I chose my courses appropriately.

This was before I had met anyone who actually did the courses. After I met people who did the courses, we chatted a lot about what courses we would do, why we would or would not do them, and whether the lecturer is nice or not. So mainly, that is how I get most of my information now, just by talking to people.

**Me:** If you could think of three skills that you have gained from the courses you have taken so far, what would you say?

**Interviewee:** Three skills from the courses I have taken so far?

**Me:** Yes, from the courses you have taken so far. The semester is still a bit early, so it can be from previous courses too.

**Interviewee:** Okay. First, I have learned how to understand what business is on campus, and I can quickly plan out an innovation. Secondly, I learned a lot about C, how to do memory allocation in C, and especially how to use the lower-level functions in C. Thirdly, I learned how to properly code in an object-oriented space, such as what the SOLID principles are, how to create good code, and how to make it readable, understandable, and not terrible.

**Me:** Do you feel that you would want another method for choosing your courses, or are you satisfied with following your friends?

**Interviewee:** I am completely dissatisfied with the current method. There is no information. It is purely based on asking people, and I wish there was an application or website for exactly this purpose. I hate talking to people about courses because it makes me so nervous, and I hate consulting the plan because there are a bunch of options where I do not know whether I should do music, art, philosophy, and so on.

I hate talking to people. I would love it if there was a place that centralized all this information.

**Me:** If there was a way to see before enrolling exactly what skills a course develops and how those skills connect to a career path, would you have used that for your last course decision?

**Interviewee:** Yes, I would have. In the last course decision, they did not explicitly tell us what was going to happen and what we were going to learn. It would be very nice to know that before doing the course, so we can plan our time at university more intentionally.

The course descriptions provide little to no information, and most of the time, you have to talk to other people, which I hate, to find out what you would get from the course. So I would love it if there was a place where you could find out what skills and objectives are in the course.

**Me:** What would stop you from using a new tool for course planning, even if it solved the problem that you just described?

**Interviewee:** Not much.

**Me:** So you think that if it existed, you would definitely use it?

**Interviewee:** I would definitely use it.

**Me:** I think that concludes our interview. Thank you so much for your time.
