**Me:** Hi, can I have around 5 to 10 minutes of your time to interview you regarding my project?

**Interviewee:** Alright.

**Me:** I have to inform you in advance that this interview will be recorded.

**Interviewee:** Yeah, that’s fine.

**Me:** First question: walk me through how you decided the courses that you wanted to take next semester. What did you actually do step by step? Did you do research, or did you just go with whatever your friends took?

**Interviewee:** I guess it was mainly looking at my courses and what they could be, going through each course and seeing the summary of what it is about, and possibly looking at past course profiles to see the assignments and get a vibe for it. Then, yeah, kind of seeing what was interesting.

**Me:** What do you think was the hardest part of the process?

**Interviewee:** Being aware of what courses are available. I would have done that previously if I knew they existed.

**Me:** When you are planning courses across your whole degree, how do you figure out what order makes sense or what options are even available to you? Is there anything you find frustrating or anything you would change?

**Interviewee:** I mean, it is just a slow process. When I was doing it, I would start with the later subjects and work backwards. But I do not necessarily know what I want to do in a few years’ time, so I guess just being aware of what the courses even are later on can be a bit of a pain.

**Me:** Can you explain or tell me at least three skills that you feel you have learned from all the courses you have taken?

**Interviewee:** Just any skills?

**Me:** Any skills. It can be content-related. For example, if you take CSSE1001, you might learn Python coding. Something like that.

**Interviewee:** Okay. Memory and processes, the networking stack, and just general Haskell programming.

**Me:** Before you enrolled in your last elective, what information do you wish you had but could not find?

**Interviewee:** I wish I had more past experiences with certain courses. Sometimes, for some of the courses I am doing, it would have been nice to know how painful they actually are.

**Me:** If you could fix one thing about how you currently research and choose courses, what would it be?

**Interviewee:** Maybe research more, I assume. Ask more people, I guess. Some way to really see public experiences with the courses.

**Me:** If there was a way to see before enrolling exactly what skills you would develop from taking a course, and how those skills connect to a career path or career options, would you have used that tool to help you decide your last course?

**Interviewee:** I think so, yeah.

**Me:** Let’s say next week there is a tool like that, and I send you a link so you can directly use it. Would you actually start using it?

**Interviewee:** Well, I only have one semester of courses left, so I kind of know what I am going to be doing. But if it was earlier in my degree, yeah, I would use it.

**Me:** Alright, I think that’s it for the questions. I do not have any more. Thank you.

**Interviewee:** Alright. Thank you.
