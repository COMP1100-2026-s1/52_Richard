# Learning Card
## HYPOTHESIS

We believed that university students often choose courses based more on convenience factors such as timetable fit, perceived difficulty, and recommendations from friends, rather than on the specific skills they want to develop. We also believed this happens because students do not clearly understand what skills each course develops or how those skills connect to future career outcomes. Therefore, we thought that a tool that maps courses to skills and career paths would help students make more intentional course selections.

## OBSERVATION

We observed from 5 student interviews that convenience does influence course selection, but it was not the main driver for most students. Many students also considered interest, relevance to future careers, and major alignment when choosing courses. However, several students said they were not fully informed before enrolling and often relied on unofficial sources such as seniors, friends, Reddit, Facebook, or general web searches to understand what a course would really be like. We also observed that most students were actually able to describe at least some of the skills they had developed from their courses, even if they were unclear about those skills before enrolling.

## LEARNING INSIGHTS

From that we learnt that our hypothesis was only partially correct. The strongest problem was not that most students are purely convenience-driven, but that many students lack clear, decision-friendly information before enrolling. Students often want to know the workload, assessment style, lecturer quality, relevance to career goals, and likely skills gained from a course, but the current information sources do not present this clearly in one place. We also learnt that regret about past course choices was weaker than expected, while uncertainty and incomplete understanding were much more common. This means the real opportunity is less about correcting “bad choices” and more about helping students make more confident and informed choices.

## THEREFORE WE WILL

Therefore we will refine our solution direction to focus less on only proving that students choose based on convenience, and more on helping students understand courses before enrolling. We will explore a tool that clearly maps courses to skills and career pathways, while also showing practical decision-making information such as workload, assessment structure, and peer or senior insights. In the next iteration, we should test whether students find more value in a course-to-skill-career mapping tool that also includes real student guidance and clearer pre-enrolment information, rather than just a simple skill map alone.
