## 2. Pain Points & Frustrations

### Vague course profiles / lack of information

- *"A lot of the course profiles are really vague... sometimes it's really unclear the difference between courses"* — Interviewee #3
- *"There's not a lot of information that I can look for... there's like a brief description of the course, but it's not in detail"* — Interviewee #6
- *"They will list out, oh, you are going to learn clinical skills, practical skills, but I don't know what exactly that is"* — Interviewee #6
- *"There are some courses that don't do a very good job at actually describing what they do cover"* — Interviewee #2

### Courses don't match expectations

- *"The course turned out it's not exactly the same as you expected"* — Interviewee #6 (expected easy, got heavy reading)
- *"It's just boring"* — Interviewee #2 (chose PSY1000 for ease, found it unfulfilling)
- *"I had never learnt that kind of stuff before... I didn't really get ready for that environment"* — Interviewee #6 (enrolled in Vet Sci course unprepared)

### Regret from uninformed decisions

- *"Maybe I will change another course"* — Interviewee #1 (would have dropped translation course if knew content beforehand)
- *"I've been hearing from friends about these courses that are really interesting, but I didn't know about it beforehand, so I kind of missed out"* — Interviewee #5
- *"I remember for about the first half of the course I was really sceptical that I was gonna enjoy it"* — Interviewee #3

### Gap between academics and real-life work

- *"Business changes too fast and you can't really fix it. What you learn in the course can't really fix into what I want to enroll in"* — Interviewee #1
- *"The work that you do at uni and in industry can vary quite a bit"* — Interviewee #2
- *"It takes time, and I need to gain more skills and experience to build up my confidence"* — Interviewee #6

## 4. What Students Need

1. **Clearer skill breakdowns per course** — not just "you'll learn design skills" but *what specific skills* (tools, techniques, methods)
2. **Better differentiation between similar courses** — especially when choosing between electives in the same category
3. **Visibility into assessments and workload style** — several students used past assessments as a proxy for course content (#3, #5)
4. **Connection between courses and career outcomes** — most students feel a gap between what they study and where they're headed
5. **Discovery of courses they didn't know about** — students miss interesting courses because information is scattered and relies on word-of-mouth (#5)
6. **Weekly topic breakdown before enrolling** — explicitly requested by #6

## 6. Unexpected Insights That Challenge Assumptions

1. **Some students are fine with convenience-driven choices** — Interviewee #4 deliberately picks easy courses during thesis semester and considers it a valid strategy (*"easy courses let you focus on other courses"*). Not all convenience = regret.

2. **The ECP isn't always the problem** — Interviewee #2 said the ECP *"kind of already provided a decent summary."* The issue is students are lured by ease and skip reading it, or some courses are well-described while others aren't. The problem is inconsistency, not a total absence of info.

3. **Word-of-mouth and social media are primary research channels** — Students rely on friends, family (#5's uncle), Reddit/social media, not institutional tools. This suggests SkillPath may need a social/community layer, not just a data tool.

4. **Students discover a course's value *after* taking it** — Interviewee #3 took astrophysics for "fun facts" and discovered techniques useful across their degree. Interviewee #6's hard elective taught a lot through forced reading. The gap isn't just pre-enrolment info — it's that students can't predict cross-disciplinary value.

5. **Confidence builds mid-course, not at selection time** — Interviewee #3 was *"sceptical for about the first half."* Students aren't just uncertain at enrolment — they remain uncertain well into the semester.

6. **Assessment items are used as a proxy for course content** — Multiple students (#3, #5) look at assessments from previous years to gauge what a course actually covers, because descriptions alone aren't enough.
