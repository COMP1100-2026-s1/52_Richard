
# SkillPath: User Interview Analysis (Muhammad Jordan Arrazi)

Here is an analysis of the five user interviews, highlighting the core similarities, shared pain points, and how their responses align with the hypotheses outlined in your SkillPath Test Card.

---

### 1. Common Themes & Similarities

| Theme | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| ***Peer-Driven Decisions*** | Across almost all interviews, friends and peers heavily influenced course selection. Multiple students stated they chose an elective simply because their friends were taking it, they didn't want to be alone in the class, or they relied on word-of-mouth recommendations from seniors. |
| ***The "Convenience" Factor*** | Validating your project's hypothesis, several students admitted to picking courses because they were perceived as "easy" or "chill." Their primary goal in these instances was to free up time for outside projects, internships, or personal life, rather than to acquire specific skills. |
| ***Informal Research Methods*** | Because official platforms lack subjective details, students consistently rely on informal channels. They use Reddit, ask friends, or "eyeball it" to figure out if a course is actually good or worth taking. |
| ***Awareness of a "Skill Gap"*** | Almost all interviewees acknowledged a significant gap between the skills they currently possess and the skills they believe they need for their desired post-graduation careers (such as Data Science or AI). |
| ***Self-Study is Expected*** | Several students noted that the university cannot teach them everything, especially in Computer Science, and that self-study is a mandatory reality to bridge their skill gaps. |

---

### 2. Major Pain Points & Frustrations

| Pain Point | Description & User Feedback |
| :--- | :--- |
| ***1. Poor Course Discovery and Navigation*** | Students expressed immense frustration with the university's official website (specifically mentioning UQ). Pain points include:<br>• Searching for electives is highly manual; students often have to "scramble" or guess course codes to find what they are looking for.<br>• Courses are not intuitively grouped by interest or career path (e.g., one student couldn't find "Computer Vision" because it was categorized under Engineering instead of Computer Science).<br>• Schedules and timetables are sometimes hidden until after the student actually enrolls. |
| ***2. Lack of Transparency Before Enrolling*** | Students struggle to find critical, practical information about courses before committing to them. They specifically wished they could see:<br>• The actual weekly workload (which is often omitted from the course profile).<br>• The quality of the professors teaching the specific class.<br>• A clear syllabus. One student mentioned showing up to class only to regret it because the syllabus wasn't available beforehand to explain what they would actually be learning. |
| ***3. The "Easy Course" Regret*** | While students seek out easy courses for convenience, it often leads to retrospective frustration. Multiple students regretted taking "chill" classes because they ultimately felt they wasted an elective slot without learning anything valuable toward their degree or career. |
| ***4. Difficulty Articulating Skills for the Future*** | Translating their university experience into a professional context is a struggle:<br>• Some lacked the confidence to map their coursework to CV-ready skills.<br>• Others stated they couldn't clearly picture their career three years down the line because their degree felt too broad and they were going in "blind."<br>• One student explicitly requested the need for workshops to periodically map out their progressing skills and interests to give them better direction. |

---

### 3. Alignment with the SkillPath Test Card

The interview data strongly supports the core problem your Test Card is trying to solve:

| Test Card Element | Interview Validation |
| :--- | :--- |
| ***Hypothesis Validation*** | The interviews confirm that students fail to clearly understand the skill sets cultivated by courses. They heavily rely on convenience factors like timetable fit and friend recommendations. |
| ***Metric 1: Convenience Driven*** | A large portion of the interviewees admitted that choosing an easy course, or following friends, was a primary driver for their electives. |
| ***Metric 2: Skill Unawareness*** | Students confirmed they often go in blind regarding the specific skills or syllabus of a course before enrolling. |
| ***Metric 3: Regret*** | Several students expressed regret over taking easy courses that provided no valuable skills, directly hitting your metric for post-selection frustration. |
| ***Product Fit*** | When introduced to the "SkillPath" concept (mapping courses to skills and career paths), the interviewees reacted positively. They noted it would be useful, especially if it tracked progress and recommended courses based on specific career goals. |
