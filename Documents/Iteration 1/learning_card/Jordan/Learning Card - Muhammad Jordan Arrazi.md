
# SkillPath: Learning Card (Muhammad Jordan Arrazi)

### SkillPath Learning Card

Here is how you can fill out your Learning Card based on the insights gathered from the five user interviews.

| Learning Card Phase | Insights & Action Items |
| :--- | :--- |
| ***We observed that …*** | • Students predominantly choose elective courses based on convenience factors—such as fitting their timetable, perceived ease, or simply because their friends are taking the class.<br>• Before enrolling, students struggle to find critical, practical information on official university platforms (like actual weekly workloads, clear syllabi, or professor quality), forcing them to rely on informal channels like Reddit, word-of-mouth, or guesswork.<br>• A significant portion of students later regret taking "chill" or "easy" courses because they realize they wasted an opportunity to learn something useful, acknowledging a massive gap between their current skills and what they need for their desired careers.<br>• Students frequently struggle with the confidence to translate their academic experiences into tangible skills for a CV or job interview. |
| ***From this we learnt that …*** | • ***Our core hypothesis is valid:*** Students do default to convenience, largely because they lack a clear understanding of how specific courses translate to career-relevant skills.<br>• ***Unexpected Learning 1:*** The problem is heavily compounded by poor course discovery UX. Students are highly frustrated by how difficult it is to manually search, filter, and group courses by interest on the official university website.<br>• ***Unexpected Learning 2:*** Students aren't just looking for a map of skills; they are desperate for transparency. They want peer reviews, workload estimations, and a clear preview of what a course actually entails before committing to it.<br>• ***Unexpected Learning 3:*** Students don't just need help *choosing* courses; they need help *tracking* their progress so they can confidently articulate their developed skills to employers upon graduation. |
| ***Therefore, we will …*** | • ***Accept our initial hypothesis*** that a tool mapping courses to skills and career paths (SkillPath) is necessary and desired by the student demographic to help them make intentional choices.<br>• ***Investigate a new hypothesis:*** We believe that if SkillPath incorporates transparent, peer-driven course metrics (like actual workload estimations and syllabus previews) alongside the skill-mapping, user adoption and trust will significantly increase.<br>• ***Iterate our solution:*** We will ensure the app includes a visual progress-tracking feature (like the mentioned "skill tree" or a milestone tracker) so students can continuously monitor their development and easily translate their progress into resume-ready skills. |
