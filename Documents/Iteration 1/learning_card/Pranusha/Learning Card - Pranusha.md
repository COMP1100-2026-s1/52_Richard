Learning Card:

HYPOTHESIS We believed that ....

University students selected courses based on their convenience factors such as, timetable fit, difficultly levels, and/or friend recommendations rather than focusing on specific skills required for their career outcomes. We believe this occurs because students do not understand the skill sets developed in each course or how these abilities align with potential career outcomes. We believed that the existence of a tool that maps courses to skills and career paths would help students make more intentional and strategic course selections.

OBSERVATION We observed ....

From interviewing five first-year university students, we observed that most students did not primarily choose courses based on convenience. Instead, they often relied on official university planning guides, the UQ website, or recommendations from friends who had already taken the courses. However, several students expressed confusion about the best order to take courses and uncertainty about degree timelines. We also observed that many students struggled to clearly identify the specific skills they had developed from their courses, often mentioning only general skills such as time management or organisation.

LEARNING INSIGHTS From that we learnt that ...

From these interviews we learnt that our original assumption was only partially correct. While convenience factors do influence some decisions, students are more concerned about course order, pathways, and understanding how courses fit together across the degree. Additionally, students often struggle to articulate the skills developed through their courses, suggesting that there is a gap in how universities communicate skill development and career connections.

THEREFORE, WE WILL Therefore we will ...

Therefore, we will refine our idea to focus more on helping students understand course timelines, pathways, and the skills developed from each course, rather than only helping them choose courses based on interests. The proposed tool will aim to visually connect courses, skills, and possible career outcomes, while also providing clear course sequences and alternative pathways to help students plan their degree more confidently.
