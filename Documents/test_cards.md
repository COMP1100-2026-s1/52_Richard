# Hypothesis

**We believe that** university students nowadays often select courses
based on convenience factors (timetable fit, perceived difficulty,
friend recommendations, etc) rather than focusing on the specific skills
they aim to develop. We believe the causation of this situation is
because students failed to understand clearly the skill sets cultivated
by each course and how these abilities align with potential career
outcomes. We believe the existence of a tool that maps courses to skills
and career paths will help students make more intentional course
selections. We propose that a tool showing how courses connect to skills
and career paths would help students to make more purposeful and
strategically course selections.

# Test

**To verify that, we will** conduct 5 -- 10 user interviews per team
member with university students. We will explore their current course
selection habits, focus entirely on understanding how they currently
choose courses and whether they experience a gap between their course
selections and skill awareness.

# Metrics

**And measure:**

- Percentage of student whose course choices are primarily driven by
  convenience rather than skill development.

- Percentage of student who say they do not know what skills a course
  will be developed on before enrolling.

- Percentage of student who express frustration or regret about past
  course choices.

- Percentage of student who struggle to express the skills they have
  developed from their degree so far.

# Criteria

**We are right if:**

- 70% or more students admit their course choices are primarily
  convenience driven.

- 60% or more students cannot clearly articulate the skills their
  course have developed.

- 50% or more students express some form of frustration or regret
  about past course selection.

# Interview Questions

**Therefore, we will ask**

- How did you decide which course to take this semester?

  - *What is the main thing that affected your decision?*

  - *Was there anything you wished to know before enrolling?*

- Walk me through the last time you had to pick an elective course,
  what did you do?

  - *Where did you research for the information of the specific
    course? Who did you ask?*

  - *Did you feel confident in your choice, or were you just going
    with it?*

- Have you ever picked a course mainly because it was "chill" or fit
  your timetable?

  - *How did that turn out?*

  - *Do you feel it was worth your time?*

- If I asked you to list the skill you have developed from your degree
  so far, how easy would it be?

  - *Give it a try...*

  - *Do you think your course taught you those? Or did you pick them
    up elsewhere?*

- Named one of your courses you finished recently, can you explain
  what skill you have developed / gained from that course?

  - *Did you know it would develop those skills before you
    enrolled?*

  - *If you know that, would you change your decision?*

- Do you have a rough idea of what job or role you want after
  graduating?

  - *Do you feel confident your current courses are leading you
    there?*

  - *Do you think there is a gap?*

- Imagine you have a time machine that can travel you back to your
  first semester but knowing what you know now, what would you do
  differently about how you choose courses?

  - *Is there a course you regret to enrol in?*

- If you could change one thing about how you choose courses at
  university, what would it be?

  - *Why?*

  - *We did ask already how they usually choose their courses, but
    we need to find out whether with the current knowledge they know
    about the course, would they still see that their choice is the
    right one for them.*

- Think about how you chose courses in your first year versus how you
  choose them now --- has what matters to you shifted, and if so, what
  caused that shift? (Follow up for non-first year student)
