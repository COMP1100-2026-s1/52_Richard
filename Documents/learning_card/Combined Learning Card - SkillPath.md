# Combined Learning Card - SkillPath (Iteration 1)

## HYPOTHESIS

**We believed that** university students often select courses based on convenience factors — such as timetable fit, perceived difficulty, and friend recommendations — rather than focusing on the specific skills they aim to develop. We believed this occurs because students do not clearly understand the skill sets cultivated by each course or how these abilities align with potential career outcomes. We believed that a tool that maps courses to skills and career paths would help students make more intentional and strategic course selections.

---

# OBSERVATION

**We observed that** our hypothesis was only partially correct. Across approximately 25 interviews conducted by five team members, the following patterns emerged:

|Pattern|Explanation|
|---|---|
|Convenience-Driven Selection|Around 14 out of 25 students admitted to convenience-driven course choices. However, results varied significantly across team members (Eddie: 6/6, Jordan: ~4/5, Brian: 2/5, William: ~1/4, Pranusha: 1/5). Convenience does influence course selections, but it was not the only reason/dominant driver for the majority of students. Many students also considered interest, relevance to their major, future career goals, and degree requirements. Several students were constrained by fixed course plans, mandatory structures, or limited elective flexibility, indicating that they had little room for convenience-based decisions in the first place.|
|Pre-enrolment Information Gaps|Students consistently struggled to find critical, practical information on official university platforms before they actually enrolled. Key gaps included actual weekly workload, assessment style, and lecturer quality. Students were forced to rely on informal channels such as friends, seniors, Reddit rather than the institutional tools. ECPs were inconsistent, some provided adequate information while others were vague, making course selection feel like a "gamble".|
|Skill Articulation|Approximately 12 out of 25 students struggled to clearly articulate the skills they had developed from their courses (Eddie: 5/6, Jordan: ~4/5, Brian: 0/5, William: ~1/4, Pranusha: 1/5), often defaulting to general skills like time management or organisation. However, some students were able to describe at least some skills from recent courses, even if they were unclear about those skills before enrolling. Students consistently lacked the confidence to translate their academic experiences into CV skills.|
|Course Pathways and the "Bigger Picture"|Students often understood the value of individual courses but struggled with understanding how courses connect to each other. For example, what order makes sense, and how their degree builds toward a career. Some students expressed confusion about the best sequence of courses and uncertainty about degree timelines.|
|Regret|Around 14 out of 25 students expressed some form of frustration, regret, or uncertainty about past course selections (Eddie: 4/6, Jordan: ~3/5, Brian: 2/5, William: ~1/4, Pranusha: 3/5). However, outright regret was weaker than expected overall; uncertainty and incomplete understanding were far more common.|

---

## LEARNING INSIGHTS

**From this we learnt that**

1. **The core hypothesis is partially validated but needs refinement.**

    - Students do lack skill-level visibility when choosing courses, and convenience plays a role.
    - However, the primary problem is not that students are lazy or purely convenience-driven. The deeper issue is that students lack clear information before enrolling and struggle to see how courses, skills, and career paths connect.

2. **Poor course discovery UX compounds the problem.**

    - Students are highly frustrated by how difficult it is to search, filter, and group courses by interest on the official university website. Courses are not intuitively grouped by career path, and critical details (timetables, workload) are sometimes hidden until after enrolment.

3. **Inconsistency in course descriptions is a major issues.**

    - Some course profiles provide sufficient information, while others are vague. The problem is inconsistency and lack of practical, decision-relevant detail.

4. **Word-of-mouth and social media are the primary research channels.**

    - Students rely heavily on friends, seniors, family, Reddit, and social media rather than institutional tools. This suggests SkillPath may need a social or community layer, not just a data tool.

5. **Students need transparency, not just skill map.**

    - They want peer reviews, workload estimations, assessment style previews, and a clear picture of what a course actually entails before committing.

6. **Students need help understanding pathways, not just individual courses.**

    - Course order, prerequisite flow, and how courses fit together across a degree were more significant concerns than the content of any single course.

7. **Convenience-driven selection is not always negative.**

    - Some students (e.g. those in their thesis semester) intentionally choose easy courses as a valid strategy to manage workload.
    - Not all convenience equals regret.

8. **The real solution is less about correcting "bad choices", is more about helping students make confident, informed choices.**

    - Outright regret is much less common than uncertainty and inadequate understanding.

---

## THEREFORE, WE WILL

**Therefore, we will** accept the refined hypothesis that students need a tool providing skill-level course information, clear pathway visibility, and practical pre-enrolment data to make more intentional course selections. We will proceed to design and prototype SkillPath with the following prioritises:

1. **Course-to-skill-to-career mapping**

    - Build core functionality that visually connects courses to specific skills and career outcomes, so students can see how their choices contribute to their goals.

2. **Pathway and progression visualisation**

    - Include features that show how courses connect across semesters, recommended course sequence, prerequisite flow, and alternative pathways.

3. **Transparent, decision-friendly course information**

    - Surface practical details such as actual workload, assessment structure, weekly topic breakdowns, and lecturer quality alongside official course descriptions.

4. **Skill progress tracking**

    - Include a visual-tracking feature so students can continuously monitor their development and translate their progress into resume-ready skills.

5. **Next Iteration Testing**

    - Test whether students find more value in a comprehensive tool that combines course-to-skill-to-career mapping with real student guidance and clearer pre-enrolment information, rather than a simple map alone.
