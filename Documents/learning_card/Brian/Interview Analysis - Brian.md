## 2. Pain Points & Frustrations

### Course information is available, but not decision-friendly

- Students often had to combine official course profiles with Google, forums, friends, seniors, Reddit, or Facebook to make a decision.
- *"I wish I had clearer information about how heavy each course actually is, not just the course description."* — Interviewee #2
- *"It is kind of hard to navigate because either you have to know the code, or you just scroll through an endless list."* — Interviewee #5

### Convenience and manageability influence course choice

- Convenience still matters, especially when students want to reduce stress or keep their semester manageable.
- *"I choose the easiest course that I can take."* — Interviewee #1
- Interviewee #3 also balanced interest with ease so the semester would not get too hard.

### Students rely heavily on seniors and peers

- Several students trusted seniors, friends, and reviews more than official descriptions.
- Interviewee #3 said their course choices changed after gaining senior connections, especially one senior whose path they now follow.

### Students want clearer connection between courses and future careers

- Some students had future goals, but were still unsure whether their current courses were fully preparing them for those roles.
- Interviewee #2 explicitly mentioned a gap between coursework and real-world experience/projects.

### First-year uncertainty and under-informed decisions

- Some students described being lost early on or not knowing enough before enrolling.
- *"In the first year, I was kind of lost. I had no clue about choosing courses."* — Interviewee #3

## 4. What Students Need

1. **Clearer workload information before enrolling** — students want to know how heavy a course actually is, not just read a generic description
2. **Better navigation and filtering of course options** — students need a simpler way to discover relevant courses without already knowing course codes
3. **A clearer link between course choice, skill development, and career outcomes** — students want to know how a course contributes to a future role
4. **Guidance from trusted people, not just formal descriptions** — peer advice, senior recommendations, and expert insights increase confidence
5. **Support for early-stage students who feel lost** — especially for students in first year or when choosing electives for the first time
6. **A way to compare courses beyond title and summary** — students care about topic, lecturer, assessment style, readings, and practical usefulness

## 6. Unexpected Insights That Challenge Assumptions

1. **Students were better at articulating their skills than expected** — all five interviewees could name at least some skills or knowledge they had developed from their degree or recent courses.

2. **Convenience was not the dominant factor for most students** — while convenience mattered, most students also considered interest, future goals, or major relevance.

3. **The bigger problem may be incomplete pre-enrolment understanding, not bad decision-making** — several students were thoughtful in how they chose courses, but still lacked clear information about workload, assessment, and skill outcomes.

4. **Students rely heavily on informal human guidance** — seniors, friends, and online reviews often played a major role in shaping confidence and direction.

5. **Regret was weaker than expected, but uncertainty was common** — most students did not strongly regret their choices, but several were still under-informed or unsure whether their courses fully supported their future goals.
