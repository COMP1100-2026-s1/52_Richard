# Hypothesis

**We believe that** university students nowadays often select courses based on convenience factors (timetable fit, perceived difficulty, friend recommendations, etc) rather than focusing on the specific skills they aim to develop. We believe the causation of this situation is because students failed to understand clearly the skill sets cultivated by each course and how these abilities align with potential career outcomes. We believe the existence of a tool that maps courses to skills and career paths will help students make more intentional course selections. We propose that a tool showing how courses connect to skills and career paths would help students to make more purposeful and strategically course selections.

# Observation

**We observed that** all 6 interviewees (100%) admitted to convenience-driven choices, $\frac{5}{6}$ (83%) struggled to articulate skills gained from their courses, and $\frac{4}{6}$ (67%) expressed frustration or regret about past course selections, which exceeding all three success criteria (70%, 60%, 50%) we defined in the test card. Student consistently described course selection as a "gamble" due to vague course profiles, relied heavily on word-of-mouth and social media rather than institutional tools, and frequently used past assessment items as a proxy for understanding course content. 

# Learning Insights

**From this we learnt that** the core hypothesis is supported, students lack skill-level visibility when choosing courses. However, we also learnt that:
- Convenience-driven course selection is not always negative -- some students (e.g. thesis-semester students) intentionally choose easy courses as a valid strategy. 
- Inconsistency in course descriptions is a major issues. Some ECPs (e.g. CSSE2310) provided sufficient information for students to understand the course before enrolling, others may be vague. 
- Word-of-mouth and social media are the primary research tools, indicating SkillPath may need a community layer, not just a data tool.

# Future Action

**Therefore, we will** accept the hypothesis that students need skill-level course information and proceed to design and prototype SkillPath. In the next iteration, we will investigate:
- How to surface specific skill breakdowns and weekly topic outlines per courses. 
- Whether a peer review or community element increases engagement and trust.
