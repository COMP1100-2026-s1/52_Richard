# Hypotheses & Experiments

## 1. Test Cards

These test cards cover all relevant hypotheses, including:

| Hypothesis Type | Description | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Old problem hypothesis | Students mainly choose courses based on convenience factors such as timetable fit, difficulty, or friends' recommendations. | Partially refuted / refined |
| Refined problem hypothesis | Students lack clear information about course skills, prerequisites, career relevance, workload, and long-term pathways. | Accepted |
| New solution hypothesis | A course-to-skill-to-career mapping tool can help students make more intentional course choices. | Accepted |
| MVP feature hypothesis | Students will value course comparison features to compare skills, workload, prerequisites, and career relevance. | Accepted |
| MVP feature hypothesis | Students will value a skill tracker that helps them explain skills gained from completed courses. | Accepted |
| Product risk hypothesis | Students will only trust the product if the information is specific, evidence-backed, and easy to understand. | Accepted as a key risk |

The test cards explain:

- the hypothesis being tested
- the experiment used
- the metrics or success criteria
- the evidence collected
- the final result
- the decision made after testing

## 4. Summary of Hypothesis Results

| Hypothesis | Evidence-Based Result | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Students mainly choose courses because of convenience. | Partially refuted / refined | Convenience appeared in interviews, but it was not the full explanation. Students also lacked clear and practical course information. |
| Students lack clear information about course skills, prerequisites, career relevance, and pathways. | Accepted | Interviews showed that students struggled to understand how courses connect to skills, future courses, and careers. |
| A course-to-skill-to-career mapping tool will help students make better course decisions. | Accepted | Iteration 2 interviews showed positive responses to the product concept. |
| Course comparison is valuable for students. | Accepted | Students wanted help comparing similar courses based on skills, workload, prerequisites, and career relevance. |
| Skill tracking and skill articulation are valuable for students. | Accepted | Many students struggled to explain skills gained from completed courses and found the idea useful for CVs or career planning. |
| Trust, information quality, and simple UX are required for adoption. | Accepted as a key risk | Some students said they would only rely on the tool if the information was accurate, evidence-backed, and easy to understand. |

---

## 5. Accepted and Refuted Hypotheses

### Refuted or Refined Hypothesis

The original hypothesis that students mainly choose courses because of convenience was partially refuted. Interview evidence showed that convenience matters, but the deeper issue is the lack of clear and practical course information.

### Accepted Hypotheses

The accepted hypotheses are:

1. Students need clearer information about course skills, prerequisites, career relevance, and pathways.
2. A course-to-skill-to-career mapping tool is useful for student course planning.
3. Course comparison is a valuable feature.
4. Skill tracking and skill articulation are valuable features.
5. Trust, data quality, and simple UX are important for adoption.
