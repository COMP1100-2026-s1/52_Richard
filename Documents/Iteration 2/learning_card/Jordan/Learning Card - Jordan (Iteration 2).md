# Learning Card - SkillPath (Iteration 2 - Jordan)

## Hypothesis

**We believe that** university students often select courses based on convenience factors rather than focusing on the specific skills they aim to develop. We propose that a tool that shows how courses connect to skills and career paths will help students make more intentional, strategic course selections.

## Observation

**We observed that** 5/5 (100%) of interviewees expressed clear interest in a tool that maps courses to skills and career paths, exceeding our 70% success criteria. Furthermore, 5/5 (100%) agreed it would influence their course selection to be more intentional, and 4/5 (80%) admitted they currently find it difficult to list or articulate the skills they have developed so far. Students frequently mentioned the vagueness of current course descriptions, the fear of "blindly enrolling," and the anxiety of navigating a tough job market without a clear link between their studies and their future.

## Learning Insights

**From this we learnt that** the core hypothesis is strongly supported. However, we also gathered critical nuanced insights:
* **Visual mapping is key:** Students want to see their degree visualized like a video game "skill tree" rather than just reading lists. 
* **Translation over just tracking:** Students need the tool not just to tell them what they will learn, but to help them *articulate* those skills for resumes and interviews. 
* **Course comparisons are critical:** A major pain point is distinguishing between electives that sound nearly identical.
* **Static mapping has limits:** Skills gained in a course can differ drastically from student to student based on their personal focus (e.g., group project roles).

## Future Action

**Therefore, we will** accept the hypothesis and proceed with the core concept of SkillPath. In the next iteration, we will investigate:
* Designing the UI to incorporate visual "skill trees" to tap into gamified motivation.
* Developing a direct "Course A vs. Course B" comparison feature.
* Exploring ways to integrate social proof (ratings, peer reviews) so students trust the tool's recommendations.
