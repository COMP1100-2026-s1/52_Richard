# Feature 3: Basic Visual Pathway (The "Mini-Tree")

## Hypothesis

We believe that university students struggle to see how individual courses fit into the bigger picture of their degree progression, making long-term planning confusing and text-heavy. We propose a "Mini-Tree" visual pathway that shows a clear, linear flow: Prerequisites → The Course → Next level courses unlocked → Potential Career. This will help students visually understand context and progression, increasing their confidence in their degree path.

## Test

To verify this hypothesis, we will conduct 3 interviews per team member with university students. Interviews will focus on how students currently map out prerequisites and future courses, and their response to the concept of visual pathways instead of text-based lists.

## Metrics

We will measure the following:
1. Current planning difficulty: How students currently track prerequisites and subsequent courses.
2. Format preference: Do students prefer visual mapping (flowcharts/trees) over standard text lists?
3. Understanding of progression: Does the visual model make the "why" of a course clearer?
4. Increased confidence: Do students feel more secure in their degree trajectory when seeing the pathway mapped out?

## Criteria

We will consider the hypothesis validated if the following criteria are met:
- 60% or more students find tracking prerequisites and future courses currently confusing or tedious.
- 70% or more students prefer the visual "Mini-Tree" format over standard text-based course lists.
- 60% or more students say seeing "next level courses unlocked" and "potential career" visually mapped out increases their motivation to take foundational courses.

## Interview Questions

1. **How do you currently keep track of your prerequisites and plan which courses you need to take next year?**
   - Do you use a spreadsheet, the university site, or just keep it in your head?
   - Have you ever accidentally missed a prerequisite and had to delay your plans?
2. **When you look at a foundational course (like a 1000-level intro class), is it always clear to you what doors that course is opening for you in the future?**
   - How does not knowing what comes next affect your motivation for that class?
3. **Imagine seeing a visual timeline or flowchart that shows: "Courses you need first" → "This Course" → "Future courses this unlocks". Would this visual format be better than reading a list?**
   - Why do you think a visual format would (or wouldn't) work better for you?
4. **How helpful is it to visually see a "Career" node explicitly placed at the very end of this course pathway?**
   - Does seeing that end goal change how you view the introductory courses?
5. **If a course didn't unlock any future courses (it was a standalone elective), would that make you less likely to take it?**
   - How important is the feeling of "progression" to you?
6. **What could make a visual pathway like this confusing rather than helpful?**
   - If a course unlocked 15 different pathways, how would you want that displayed without getting overwhelmed?
