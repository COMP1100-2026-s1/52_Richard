# Feature 1: The "Skill & Career" Course Card

## Hypothesis

We believe that university students struggle to understand the practical value of courses because current course profiles are vague and filled with academic jargon. We propose a redesigned course card that strips away the fluff and explicitly lists 3-5 highly specific skills (e.g., "Python Data Analysis") and 2-3 direct job titles those skills map to. This feature will provide immediate clarity, directly addressing student frustrations with vague descriptions, and help them confidently align course choices with their professional goals.

## Test

To verify this hypothesis, we will conduct 3 interviews per team member with university students. Interviews will focus on how students currently interpret official university course descriptions and their response to a prototype of a highly specific, skill-and-career-focused course card.

## Metrics

We will measure the following:
1. Current frustration: How often students feel uncertain about what a course actually teaches based on current descriptions.
2. Clarity improvement: Whether students find the proposed card significantly clearer than existing university profiles.
3. Decision impact: Whether knowing exact skills and job titles would change a student's likelihood to enroll in a course.
4. Trust: What factors make students trust the skills and careers listed on our card.

## Criteria

We will consider the hypothesis validated if the following criteria are met:
- 70% or more students express that current official course descriptions are too vague or unhelpful.
- 80% or more students agree that the "Skill & Career" card provides a clearer understanding of the course's value.
- 60% or more students state that seeing specific skills and job titles would heavily influence their enrollment decisions.

## Interview Questions

1. **Have you ever read a university course description and still felt like you had no idea what actual skills you would walk away with?**
   - How did that uncertainty affect your decision to take or skip that course?
2. **Imagine a course profile that skips the long paragraphs and instead lists exactly 3-5 concrete skills (like "Agile Methodology" or "UI/UX Wireframing"). How would that change your browsing experience?**
   - Is there a specific type of skill you would be looking for?
3. **If this course card also explicitly listed 2-3 job titles (e.g., "Data Analyst") that highly value these skills, how helpful would that be?**
   - Would seeing a job title you *don't* want make you avoid the course?
4. **What would make you trust the skills listed on this new card over the official university course page?**
   - If the skills listed were too broad (like "communication"), would you still find it useful?
5. **Would knowing the exact, practical skill payoff make you more willing to enroll in a course that is known to be difficult?**
   - Why or why not?
6. **If you were designing this course card, what is the absolute most important piece of information it must have at the very top?**
