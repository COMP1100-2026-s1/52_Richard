# Competitor Analysis: SkillPath


## Our value proposition

Our project, **SkillPath**, aims to help UQ students with elective flexibility choose courses intentionally, by making clear which skills each course develops and how those skills connect to career pathways. We believe our unique value proposition is combining standardised, decision-friendly course information with an explicit course-to-skill-to-career mapping at the moment of enrolment, something that no existing UQ tool, peer channel, or skills platform currently does.

## Competitors selected

We chose six competitors that together cover the three things our customers told us they currently do when choosing courses: (1) consult official UQ tools (Programs and Courses, Program Planner), (2) seek peer signal (StudentVIP, Reddit r/UQ), and (3) try to address the underlying skill / career gap themselves (LinkedIn Learning, UQ Employability Award). SkillPath itself is included as the final row for direct comparison.

## Comparison table

| Competitor | Target market | Key value propositions | Weaknesses | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **UQ Programs and Courses website (incl. ECPs)** *Indirect competitor* | All UQ undergraduate and postgraduate students. The default starting point most students currently use when picking electives. | • Authoritative, official source for program rules, course lists, prerequisites and assessment.<br>• Every course links to an Electronic Course Profile (ECP) with weekly topics, assessment, and texts.<br>• Free, always available, and fully integrated with my.UQ. | • Information is fragmented across program pages, course lists, and individual ECPs.<br>• ECP quality is highly inconsistent: some are detailed, others are extremely vague (a top pain raised in our interviews).<br>• No skill-level or career-pathway view, so students cannot see what a course actually develops.<br>• Timetables and full syllabi often unavailable until after enrolment opens.<br>• No peer review, no comparison view, no personalisation. | This is the baseline our product has to be clearly better than. We do not need to replace it; instead we layer on top of it (skill mapping, peer data, comparison) so students still trust UQ as the source of truth. |
| **UQ Program Planner / Faculty Study Planners** *Direct competitor* | Current UQ students planning their full degree, especially dual-degree and structured-program students. Used heavily by faculty advisors. | • Lets students lay out courses across semesters and validates against program rules and majors/minors.<br>• Plans can be saved, shared via URL, and reviewed by UQ academic advisors.<br>• Specialised faculty planners (e.g. Science, BEL) cover dual-degree edge cases. | • Purely a rules / sequencing tool: it answers "is this plan valid?" rather than "is this plan good for me?".<br>• No skill or career-outcome information attached to courses.<br>• The main central Program Planner has been intermittently suspended, which has hurt student trust.<br>• UI is form-heavy and not built for elective discovery or browsing by interest. | Closest direct competitor on the planning side. Strong opportunity to integrate with or complement it: Program Planner answers "can I take this?", SkillPath answers "should I?". |
| **StudentVIP (UQ subject reviews)** *Direct competitor* | UQ students looking for honest peer opinions on a course before enrolling. The de facto "unofficial" review channel for many students alongside Reddit. | • Real student-written reviews and star ratings on UQ courses.<br>• Surfaces the kind of word-of-mouth signal (lecturer quality, workload, vibe) that students said they actively seek.<br>• Free to read, fast to scan. | • Coverage is uneven, with many courses having only a handful of reviews or none at all.<br>• Reviews are unstructured prose; no consistent breakdown of skills, workload hours, or assessment style.<br>• No connection between reviews and skills, careers, or degree progression.<br>• Self-selection bias: students with strong opinions (often negative) are over-represented. | A community / peer-review layer is something SkillPath should learn from rather than try to beat outright. Opportunity: structure peer feedback (workload hours, skills used, assessment fairness) so it is comparable across courses, not just a feed of opinions. |
| **Reddit r/UQ and similar student forums** *Indirect competitor* | Students wanting honest, anonymous, real-time peer advice on courses, electives, and lecturers. | • High-trust, conversational advice, since students believe other students more than the website.<br>• Covers context the official site never will: "is this lecturer still teaching?", "is this elective still chill?".<br>• Free and active; new threads daily during enrolment periods. | • Unstructured and not reliably searchable; answers are buried in comment threads.<br>• Advice is anecdotal, dated, and contradictory; no quality control.<br>• Nothing connects course choices to skills or career outcomes.<br>• Several interviewees said they used Reddit but still felt under-informed afterwards. | This is the channel SkillPath most directly displaces, because it is what students currently use to fill the information gap. Validates the demand: students are willing to spend real time hunting for this information. |
| **LinkedIn Learning** *Indirect competitor* | University students and early-career professionals who want career-aligned skills outside their degree. Free for UQ students through institutional access. | • Explicit skill paths and role guides mapped to in-demand careers.<br>• Skill assessments and certificates that can be displayed on a LinkedIn profile.<br>• Huge catalogue (20,000+ courses) updated regularly. | • Operates entirely outside the UQ degree, so it does not tell students which UQ courses build which skills.<br>• Solves the "build a skill" problem, not the "choose the right elective" problem.<br>• Certificates are seen by some employers as lower-signal than university coursework. | Important indirect competitor because it is where students go when they feel their degree is not building career-relevant skills. Reinforces our hypothesis: students actively want skill-to-career visibility, they just cannot get it from UQ today. |
| **UQ Employability Award / Careers and Employability** *Indirect competitor* | UQ students wanting structured help articulating skills and building employability. Tied to extra-curricular activity, not course selection. | • Official UQ-endorsed framework for reflecting on and articulating skills.<br>• Includes the SEAL reflection process and access to careers advisors.<br>• Recognised on transcripts and useful for resume / interview preparation. | • Focused on extra-curricular activities, not on courses or electives.<br>• Mostly self-reflective; it does not tell students which UQ courses develop which skills, or sequence them toward a career.<br>• Application-based, with deadlines and interviews; high friction, low coverage of total student body.<br>• Doesn't help during the enrolment moment, which is when our interviewees said they needed help most. | Solves a related but adjacent problem (articulating skills you already have) rather than ours (choosing courses to build the right skills). Possible partnership angle: SkillPath data on course-level skills could feed directly into Employability Award reflections and resume writing. |
| **SkillPath (our product)** *Our product* | UQ students with elective flexibility who lack clarity on how courses connect to skills and careers. Especially: students without a course plan, dual-degree students, career-changers, and final-year students preparing for job applications. | • Course-to-skill-to-career mapping engine that visualises what each course actually develops.<br>• Standardised, decision-friendly course information layered on top of existing ECPs (workload, assessment style, peer signal).<br>• Side-by-side course comparison so students can choose intentionally instead of by convenience.<br>• Skill progress tracker that helps students articulate what they have learnt for resumes and interviews. | • New product, with no brand recognition or trust yet at UQ.<br>• Skill mapping accuracy depends on quality of underlying data (ECPs, peer input) which is itself inconsistent.<br>• Will need ongoing curation; risks becoming stale if not maintained.<br>• Coverage starts narrow (one or a few faculties) so early users may not find their courses on day one. | Our differentiator is the combination no competitor offers: official course data, structured peer signal, and explicit skill/career mapping, all at the moment of course selection. Strategic implication from this analysis: do not try to out-compete Reddit on community or LinkedIn Learning on content. Win on "intentional course selection" specifically. |

## Key insights and strategic implications

| Insight | Strategic implication |
|---|---|
| No single competitor connects UQ courses to skills and careers. | This is the gap SkillPath should own. Our messaging and prototype should lead with the mapping, not with planning or reviews. |
| Students already work hard to fill the information gap (Reddit, StudentVIP, friends). | Demand is real. The competition is not "do nothing"; it is a fragmented set of unofficial channels we can replace with one structured tool. |
| Official UQ tools are trusted but information-sparse; peer channels are information-rich but unstructured. | We should sit between the two: ingest official data, structure peer signal, and present both consistently. We don't need to replace Programs and Courses; we layer onto it. |
| LinkedIn Learning and the Employability Award show students value skill / career framing, but neither links it to UQ coursework. | Reinforces our core hypothesis from Iteration 1 interviews. Possible future partnership angle with UQ Employability so SkillPath data feeds resume / reflection workflows. |
| Several interviewees flagged that they want simple, non-overwhelming UI with concrete (not generic) skill descriptions. | UX quality is a real competitive moat here, because every competitor in this list is either bare-bones (UQ tools), text-heavy (forums), or sprawling (LinkedIn Learning). |
